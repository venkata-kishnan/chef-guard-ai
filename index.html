Problem Statement & Commercial Restaurant Impact

In commercial restaurant kitchens and dining chains, food safety non-conformance, customer plate returns from poor cooking/burns, and raw ingredient spoilage cost billions annually. Human quality check in busy kitchens is subjective and prone to fatigue. ChefGuard AI establishes an autonomous optical gate ensuring zero defective dishes reach guests.

5-Stage System Pipeline

Stage 1: Optical Acquisition: Captures high-res plate image → Color normalization.
Stage 2: Dynamic Corner Subtraction: Samples 4 corners → Computes background color vector $\mathbf{C}_{bg}$ → Segments food from plate/counter.
Stage 3: Multi-Spectral Defect & Doneness Extraction: CIELAB $L^*$ luminance decay detects charring & rot; Discrete 2D Laplacian operator detects crisp texture vs wilted greens.
Stage 4: Computer Vision Metrology & Sizing: Diameter, volume, and portion sphericity.
Stage 5: Culinary Verdict & HACCP Clearance: Plating score, service status, and printable audit slips.

Mathematical Formulations

1. Background Color Space Distance:

ΔE = √[ (R - R_bg)² + (G - G_bg)² + (B - B_bg)² ]

2. Discrete Laplacian Spatial Texture Operator:

∇²I(x, y) = [∂²I / ∂x²] + [∂²I / ∂y²]

3. Freshness & Doneness Score:

Score = max(5.0, min(99.0, 100 - 2.2*Defect% - Char_Penalty - Mold_Penalty - Roughness_Penalty))

Top 10 Viva Exam Defense Q&A

Q1: How does the system distinguish a golden-baked pizza crust from a burnt one?

Answer: Golden crust has high saturation ($S > 110$) and medium lightness ($L \approx 130-180$). Burnt spots exhibit severe luminance drops ($L < 40$) and low chromaticity, triggering charring defect penalties.

Q2: How is leafy green salad wilting detected?

Answer: Crisp lettuce exhibits high turgidity and uniform chlorophyll green hue. As leaves wilt, edge oxidation shifts hue towards brown/yellow and increases Laplacian edge roughness.
